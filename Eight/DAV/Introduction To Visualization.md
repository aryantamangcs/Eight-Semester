
## Introduction to Visual Perception

#### 1.1 What is Visual Perception?

Visual Perception is the process by which your brain interprets and makes meaning from what your eyes see.

Your eyes collect light and send signals to the brain.
Now in your brain those signals become object, depth, motion and meaning.

**Visual** -> Your eyes collecting information
**Visual Perception** -> Your brain interpreting and making meaning out of visual information.


#### 1.2 What is Visual System ?

A system where multiple parts work together to convert light into meaningful information is called visual system.

Light -> Eye -> Electrical Signals -> Brain Processing -> Meaningful Information(Perception).

1. Light enters the eye
2. Cornea — the first focusing layer (bend (refract) incoming light and performs most of the eye focusing)
3. Pupil and iris — controlling brightness(After passing the cornea, light reaches the pupil The iris (colored part) controls pupil size.)
4. Lens — fine focus adjustment (change shape to focus at different distances)
5. Retina — converting light into neural signals (this is the most fascinating part of visual system)
6. Optic nerve — transmitting information (signals leaves via this nerve from eye to brain)

#### 1.3 Simple Model of Perceptual Processing

We know, eyes receives light and sends signals to the brain and brain interprets the signals into meaning.

Perceptual processing model is the process of how brain interprets the signal into meaning. This model includes three phases:

***Visual Perception (Seeing)*** : Brain takes signals and identifies basic features such as color, depth, shadow, position, shape, size.

***Pattern Recognition (Interpretation)*** : The brain organizes what it sees such as color, position, size, shape into meaningful patterns such as trends, comparisons, clusters, outliers etc.

Outlier-> Different from the rest.

***Cognitive Processing (understanding and decision making)*** :  The viewer combines the visual patterns which ultimately leads to drawing conclusions, making decisions , solving problems etc.

#### 1.4 Why is visual perception important?

Visual perception is very crucial because it allows people to interact with the world around them. And human society is so strongly biased towards visual experiences.

#### 1.5 Challenges of Visual perception.

Anything that blocks us from properly seeing, interpreting and understanding the visuals are the challenges of visual perception.

- Color blindness (Red and Green are most common)
- Visual Stress (Not being able to see properly such as too much brightness, flickering screens)

#### 1.6 What do you mean by Preattentive Processing?

Preattentive processing is the brain's ability to quickly and automatically detect certain visual features before conscious attention is focused on them.

Common preattentive attributes

The brain quickly notices differences in:

Size (one large circle among small circles)
Shape (one square among circles)
Orientation (one tilted line among vertical lines)
Position (an object placed higher or lower)
Length (one bar is longer than the others)
Motion (a moving object among stationary ones)

#### 1.7 What is Stroop Effect ?

The **Stroop effect** is a psychological phenomenon in which **it takes longer and requires more mental effort to identify the color of a word when the word's meaning conflicts with its color**.

It demonstrates that the brain automatically reads familiar words, even when you are trying to ignore their meaning.

### Example

Look at these words and try to **say the color of the text**, not the word itself:

```
RED     (printed in blue ink)
GREEN   (printed in red ink)
BLUE    (printed in green ink)
```

The correct answers are:

- "RED" printed in **blue** → **Blue**
- "GREEN" printed in **red** → **Red**
- "BLUE" printed in **green** → **Green**

Most people find this difficult because their brain automatically reads the word ("RED") while they are trying to identify the ink color (blue).
