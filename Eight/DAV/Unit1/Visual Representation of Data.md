The process of converting numerical or categorical data into visual forms so that people can understand quickly and make better decisions is called visual representation of data.


## Why Visual Representation is important?

- Easy Understanding
- Can make better decisions
- Detect hidden patterns
- Shows comparison between values
- Help detect errors and outliers.

## Objective of Visual Representation?

- Present data clearly
- Support decision making
- Compare values
- Reduce complexity

## Types of data

Understanding the type of data helps you to choose suitable visualization.

#### 1.1 Categorical Data
The data which represent categories such as gender, country, department etc

**Suitable Visualization** -> Pie Chart, Bar Chart

#### 1.2 Numerical Data
The data which represents numbers such as Height, Weight, Salary etc.

**Suitable Visualization** -> Histogram, Line Chart

#### 1.3 Ordinal Data
The data which represents categories with order such as Poor, Average, Good, Excellent.

**Suitable Visualization** -> Ordered bar chart

#### 1.4 Time Series Data
The data collected over time represents time series data. Such as Jan sales 20, Feb sales 30, March sales 40.

**Suitable Visualization** -> Line Chart



***The main purpose of Histogram is to show frequency distribution of continuous data***

***The main purpose of scatter plot is to show relationship between two numerical values*** (Very important)

## General Rules

- Title
- Legend
- Direction

Title -> A short sentence or phrase at the top of the chart, that tells what the chart is about.

Legend -> A small guide that explains the colors, symbols or patterns used in the charts.

Direction -> The way information flows or is organized.

## What is Graph Distortion? 

The misleading presentation of data that causes the viewers to interpret information incorrectly.

#### Causes of Graph Distortion
##### 1. Truncated Y-Axis (Most Common)

The graph does not start the Y-axis at zero, making small differences appear much larger.

##### Example

Actual Sales:

| Company | Sales |
| ------- | ----- |
| A       | 95    |
| B       | 100   |

The actual difference is only **5 units**, but if the Y-axis starts at **90 instead of 0**, Company B's bar looks much taller than Company A's.

**Problem:** Exaggerates differences.

##### 2. Missing Labels or Units

Graphs without axis labels or measurement units.

Example:

```
Sales ↑
|
|
+---------
```

No indication of whether sales are in dollars, rupees, thousands, or millions.

**Problem:** Causes confusion

##### 3. Too Many Categories

Displaying too many bars or pie slices.

Example:

A pie chart with 20 slices.

**Problem:**

- Hard to read
- Difficult to compare values
- Information overload


#### 4. Misleading Colors

Using colors that suggest incorrect meaning.

Example:

- Green for losses
- Red for profits

or using similar colors for different categories.

**Problem:** Creates confusion or wrong interpretation.
