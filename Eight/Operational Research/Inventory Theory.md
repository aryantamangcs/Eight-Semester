Inventory is the stock of resources used in an organization. It is idle resources, but usable.

## What is Inventory System?

A set of policies and controls that monitor and evaluate the levels of inventory and the determines the following questions:
- What level of inventory should be maintained
- When should stock be reordered.
- How large orders should be placed.

An inventory system is basically a process whereby a business keeps track of the goods and material it has available.
## Importance of Inventory?

- Smooth flow of work.
- Provide and maintain good customer service.
- Increases ROI (Return On Investment)
- Protection against future uncertainties.
## Inventory Costs?

For the maximum profit of an organization -> Management should focus on minimization of inventory cost.

It is also known as procurement cost.

The cost which is incurred for keeping stocks in store is carrying cost. Like rent, electricity etc.

## Types of Inventory

#### 1. Raw Materials
Raw materials are those items or resources used in manufacturing process to produce components or finished products.

- **Direct Raw Materials:** Items directly incorporated into the final product.
    - _Example (Automobile):_ Steel sheets, rubber, glass, and aluminum used by **Toyota** to assemble cars.
    - _Example (Bakery):_ Flour, sugar, yeast, and butter held by a local bakery before baking begins.
- **Indirect Raw Materials:** Items required for production that do not end up in the final physical product.
    - _Example:_ Lubricants for machines, cleaning solvents, or disposable gloves used in a chemical manufacturing facility.

#### 2. Work-in-Process
WIP refers to items that have entered the production process but are not yet completed. This includes raw materials undergoing transformation, partially assembled goods, or items waiting on the assembly line.

- _Example (Electronics):_ Circuit boards that have been soldered with chips but are awaiting installation into phone casings at an **Apple** manufacturing partner's facility.
- _Example (Apparel):_ Cut fabric pieces that have been partially stitched into jackets but are still missing zippers and buttons.

#### 3. Finished Goods
These are fully manufactured items that have passed quality checks and are ready for sale and shipment to distributors, retailers, or directly to consumers.

- _Example (Retail/E-commerce):_ Packaged shoes stored in an **Nike** distribution center ready to fulfill customer orders.
- _Example (Consumer Goods):_ Bottled sodas sitting on the shelves of a **Coca-Cola** warehouse.

## Types of Inventory System

## 1. Continuous (Perpetual) Inventory System

A **continuous inventory system** updates inventory records **instantly and automatically** every time an item is bought, sold, returned, or manufactured. It provides a real-time view of exact stock levels, Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), and inventory valuation without needing to wait for a physical count.

### How It Works:

- Uses technologies like **barcodes, RFID tags, and Point of Sale (POS) software**.
    
- When a customer purchases an item, scanning the barcode immediately subtracts that item from the central database and records the sale.
    
- Physical inventory audits are still performed (usually once a year or via cycle counting) to check for shrinkage, theft, or damage, but daily operations rely on the automated system.
    

### Real-Life Examples:

- **Amazon / E-commerce Warehouses:** When you buy a pair of headphones on Amazon, the inventory system updates in real time. If there was only 1 item left, it instantly shows "Out of Stock" to other buyers worldwide.
    
- **Supermarket Chains (e.g., Walmart, Target):** When a cashier scans a carton of milk at checkout, the system instantly logs the sale, deducts one unit of milk from stock, and can trigger an automated purchase order if stock drops below a reorder threshold.
